Privacy policy

Last updated: November 7, 2026

This page explains what personal data mariohdz.com handles, why, and what you can do about it. In short: the site doesn’t use cookies, doesn’t track you and only receives what you choose to send me.

Who’s responsible

Mario Hernández is responsible for the personal data handled through this website. You can reach me at contact@mariohdz.com about anything on this page.

What I collect and why

  • Forms and email. When you write through the contact form, I receive your name, your email address, the topic and the project you pick, and your message. When you use the guided form on Start a project, I receive your name and email address and the answers you choose to give: the projects, what you already have, the details and the timing, what you’d like from me, whether you’d rather talk by email or on a call (and, for a call, the time of day and the time zone you pick), how you found the site, and your business, website and message if you add them. It never asks for passwords. To catch typos, both forms check that the domain of your address can receive email, by asking Cloudflare’s public resolver about the domain alone. If you email me directly, I receive whatever you include. I use it only to reply and, if we decide to work together, to plan and carry out the project. The legal basis is taking the steps you ask for before a possible agreement and, if we reach one, the agreement itself (Article 6(1)(b) of the GDPR).
  • Visit statistics. Cloudflare Web Analytics counts visits and shows which pages are read. It doesn’t use cookies, doesn’t store anything on your device and doesn’t build a profile of you; I only see aggregated figures. The legal basis is my legitimate interest in knowing how the site is used (Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR).
  • Technical data. Like any website, the servers that deliver these pages handle technical data such as your IP address and browser type to send you the page and protect the site from abuse. To keep the contact form from being flooded, it counts the messages that come from each IP address for one minute; the count isn’t kept after that. I don’t use any of this to identify you.
  • Form records. Each time the form is sent, a short note of what happened (whether the message went out, was rejected or was blocked, plus its language, topic and project and, for the guided form, the projects and the kind of request) is kept for seven days so I can spot problems. It doesn’t include your name, your email address, your message or your IP address. The legal basis is my legitimate interest in keeping the form working and protected (Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR).
  • Your theme choice. If you switch between light and dark mode, your browser remembers the choice in its local storage. It stays on your device and is never sent to me.

Cookies

This site doesn’t use cookies, which is why you won’t see a cookie banner.

Who else is involved

I don’t sell your data or use it for advertising. To run the site I rely on these providers, which process data on my behalf:

  • Cloudflare, Inc. (United States) hosts the site, delivers the contact form messages, keeps the form records and provides the visit statistics. Transfers to the United States rely on Cloudflare’s certification under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework.
  • Proton AG (Switzerland) hosts my email inbox. The European Commission recognizes that Switzerland offers an adequate level of data protection.

How long I keep it

I keep messages for as long as it takes to reply and follow up on your request. If we work together, they become part of the project’s records for as long as the work and any legal obligations require. Otherwise, I delete them once they’re no longer needed. The form records are deleted automatically after seven days.

Your rights

You can ask me for a copy of your personal data, or ask me to correct it, delete it, limit how I use it or send it to you or someone else in a portable format. You can also object to how I use it. Write to contact@mariohdz.com and I’ll reply within one month at the latest, usually much sooner.

If you think I haven’t handled your data properly, you can complain to the data protection authority in the country where you live or work.

Changes

If this policy changes, I’ll update this page and the date at the top.
