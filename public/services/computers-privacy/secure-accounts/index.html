Why it matters

How accounts get opened, and how to keep them closed.

Most break-ins don’t need clever hacking. They start with a password that was reused, guessed or shared, and an account with nothing else standing in the way.

  • Passwords

    One strong password per account.

    A password manager creates and remembers a different, strong password for every account, so one leak never opens the rest.

  • Two steps

    A password alone isn’t enough.

    Two-step sign-in asks for a code or a key from your phone, so a stolen password can’t open the door on its own.

  • Access

    It follows your team.

    Each person gets only what their work needs, shared through the manager, and loses it the day they leave.

Business email compromise, when someone takes over or fakes a company’s email, led to $2.77 billion in reported losses in the U.S. in 2024, according to the FBI’s annual Internet Crime Report.

Start with email.

Whoever controls your inbox can reset almost every other password, so it’s the first account to protect, and the one to protect best.

FAQ

Questions about secure accounts.

Which password manager do you use?

Usually Bitwarden, which is open source, works on every device and has plans for teams. If your team already uses another good one, we keep it.

What if someone loses their phone?

That’s what the recovery codes are for. They’re stored safely during setup, so nobody is locked out of an account for good.

Will this slow my team down?

Usually the opposite. The manager fills in passwords on its own, and two-step sign-in asks for a code only now and then on a trusted device.

Do I need to give you my passwords?

No. You invite me to your team’s password manager while it’s set up, passwords stay inside it, never in an email or a chat, and you remove me when we’re done.

What does it cost each month?

Your password manager’s plan, per person each month. For one person, Bitwarden also has a free plan.
