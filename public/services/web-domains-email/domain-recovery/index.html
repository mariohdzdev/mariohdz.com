Why it matters

Why it’s worth fixing now.

Your domain is the address of both your website and your email. Whoever controls the account it’s registered in controls both, whatever the paperwork says.

  • Control

    One login holds everything.

    The registrar account decides where your website and email point. Whoever has it can move them, or let them lapse.

  • Renewal

    A date nobody watches.

    Domains renew once a year. When the reminder goes to someone who left, your site and your email can go offline together.

  • Proof

    Easier while it’s calm.

    Most recoveries take a few emails and some proof that the business is yours. It’s simplest before anything breaks.

Check whose name your domain is in.

A public lookup, like WHOIS or RDAP, usually shows your registrar, and old invoices usually show who paid for the domain. If it isn’t you, fix it while it’s easy.

Process

How it comes back.

  1. 01

    Find

    Where your domain is registered, who holds each account and when everything renews, all written down.

  2. 02

    Ask

    Whoever holds it gets a clear, friendly request to hand it over. When that isn’t possible, the registrar’s own process starts.

  3. 03

    Move

    Each account moves into one in your name, with two-step sign-in, and the domain can move to a registrar you choose.

  4. 04

    Lock

    Renewals become automatic, the transfer lock goes on and you get your keys: every account and date in one document.

FAQ

Questions about getting your domain back.

What if the previous person won’t answer?

Registrars have their own process for owners who can’t reach whoever set up the account. It takes longer and asks for proof, and you’ll know the steps and timing before we start.

Will my website or email go offline?

No. Everything keeps running while the accounts change hands, and nothing moves until the new accounts are ready.

Do I need to know my registrar?

No. Finding it is the first step, along with every account connected to your domain.

Can my domain stay where it is?

Yes. It can stay with the same registrar, in an account in your name, or move to one you prefer.

What does it cost besides the project?

Only what your registrar charges for a renewal or a transfer, usually the price of one year of the domain.
